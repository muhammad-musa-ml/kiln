# Clipping earnings case: reconcile views, time and payment states

Researched 2026-11-07.

## Strengths and best use
The case study supplies a concrete workflow to investigate. It separates editing, manual uploading, a submission deadline, views, and pending earnings. Its strongest lesson is that operational timing and approval rules can dominate the value of a popular clip.

## Independent checks
The stated $464 divided by 497,000 views equals about $0.934 per thousand total views. That is an effective observed ratio from the creator's figures. Qualifying geography, caps, fees, and rejected submissions can change the contractual payout. Repeating $464 over two hours for ten hours every week yields $120,640 annually, but the extrapolation requires consistent demand, budget, approval, and view performance that the reel does not establish.

Content Rewards currently documents the 30-minute submission window, campaign-dependent minima and caps, and settlement conditions. Its fee page also includes exceptions for certain per-post and retainer campaigns. [Official mechanics](https://help.contentrewards.com/en/articles/15319790-how-content-rewards-works)

## Reproduce the measurement
Use a campaign that explicitly permits the source material. Track sourcing, editing, caption review, posting, submission, rework, and payment reconciliation. Save posted and submitted times separately. Compare every clip in the cohort, including zero-view and rejected clips, over the same observation window. Report settled net earnings divided by all labor hours.

## Reusable prompt
```text
Audit this clipping experiment using the whole cohort. Reconcile total views with qualifying views, campaign caps, fees, pending amounts, and settled payouts. Include all work time and failed submissions. Show which assumptions drive the annual projection.
```
